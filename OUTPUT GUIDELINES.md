\# output\_guide.md

\#\# Purpose

This document defines:

1\. The structure of every concept generated by the Culinary Intelligence System.  
2\. The thinking framework the AI should use when generating each section.

The goal is consistency.

The AI should not generate free-form essays.

Every recommendation should follow the same structure so concepts can be compared, evaluated, prioritized, and refined over time.

\---

\# Core Principle

The purpose of the output is NOT to generate recipes.

The purpose of the output is to generate R\&D concepts worth prototyping.

The output should help answer:

\- Why should this concept exist?  
\- Why now?  
\- Why would customers care?  
\- What assumptions should be tested?

\---

\# Output Structure

\#\# 1\. Concept Name

\#\#\# Purpose

Create a memorable working title for the concept.

\#\#\# AI Guidelines

\- Short and descriptive.  
\- Avoid overly creative marketing names.  
\- Focus on clarity.  
\- Should help internal teams remember the concept.

\#\#\# Good Examples

\- Crispy Lamb & Nduja Rice  
\- Charred Eggplant with Hot Honey Labneh  
\- Smoked Beef Cheek Flatbread

\#\#\# Bad Examples

\- Mediterranean Dreamscape  
\- Lamb Reimagined  
\- Fire & Soul

\---

\#\# 2\. One-Line Concept

\#\#\# Purpose

Describe the concept in a single sentence.

\#\#\# AI Guidelines

Must answer:

"What is the dish?"

Structure:

Hero \+ key flavor driver \+ format

\#\#\# Example

Slow-cooked lamb shoulder finished crispy and served over whipped labneh rice with nduja oil and herbs.

\---

\#\# 3\. Brief Summary

\#\#\# Purpose

Provide context for reviewers.

\#\#\# AI Guidelines

Summarize the original brief.

Include:

\- Category  
\- Strategic Role  
\- Familiarity  
\- Discovery  
\- Pantry Assets  
\- Opportunity  
\- Desired Feeling  
\- Constraints

Do not reinterpret.

Simply summarize.

\---

\#\# 4\. Concept Breakdown

\#\#\# Purpose

Help teams quickly understand the building blocks of the idea.

\#\#\# Structure

\#\#\#\# Hero

The main focus of the dish.

\#\#\#\# Key Flavor Drivers

The ingredients or pantry assets that define the flavor.

\#\#\#\# Key Textures

The primary texture experiences.

\#\#\#\# Key Contrast

The most important contrast or tension in the dish.

\#\#\# AI Guidelines

Do not describe every ingredient.

Focus on the few elements that make the concept interesting.

\#\#\# Example

Hero:  
Lamb Shoulder

Key Flavor Drivers:  
Nduja  
Labneh  
Lemon

Key Textures:  
Crispy  
Creamy  
Crunchy

Key Contrast:  
Hot nduja against cool labneh

\---

\#\# 5\. Presentation & Service Moment

\#\#\# Purpose

Describe how the dish comes to life.

\#\#\# AI Guidelines

Think beyond plating.

Consider:

\- What guests see first  
\- What guests photograph  
\- What servers do  
\- What creates memorability

\#\#\# Questions

\- What creates the visual impact?  
\- Is there a reveal moment?  
\- Is there a sharing moment?  
\- Is there a table-side moment?

\#\#\# Example

The lamb arrives whole and crispy.

The server pulls apart the crust tableside, exposing the tender interior and releasing aromatic nduja oil.

\---

\#\# 6\. Why It Could Win

\#\#\# Purpose

Explain why this concept deserves attention.

\#\#\# AI Guidelines

DO NOT describe the dish.

Instead explain:

Why would guests order this?

Possible angles:

\- Trend relevance  
\- Menu gap  
\- Emotional appeal  
\- Pantry leverage  
\- Social media appeal  
\- Strategic differentiation

\#\#\# Example

Most viral comfort dishes in Jakarta borrow from Japanese or Korean flavor systems.

This concept delivers the same creamy, indulgent experience through Mediterranean ingredients and techniques, reinforcing Salted Olive's identity while remaining highly approachable.

\---

\#\# 7\. Feasibility Notes

\#\#\# Purpose

Identify operational opportunities and risks.

\#\#\# AI Guidelines

Focus only on meaningful considerations.

Structure:

\#\#\#\# Existing Assets Leveraged

Examples:

\- Existing pantry items  
\- Existing sauces  
\- Existing preparations  
\- Existing supplier relationships

\#\#\#\# Watchouts

Examples:

\- Cost  
\- Consistency  
\- Complexity  
\- Shelf life  
\- Service challenges

\#\#\# Example

Existing Assets Leveraged:  
\- Labneh  
\- Nduja Sauce

Watchouts:  
\- Lamb crisping consistency  
\- Portion cost control

\---

\#\# 8\. Experiment Focus

\#\#\# Purpose

Identify the most important assumptions to test.

\#\#\# AI Guidelines

This is one of the most important sections.

Focus on uncertainty.

Do not generate generic survey questions.

Identify the 2-4 assumptions that determine success.

\#\#\# Questions

Ask:

"If this assumption is wrong, would the concept fail?"

If yes, include it.

\#\#\# Example

Validate:

1\. Does the tableside reveal create enough excitement?  
2\. Does the dish feel indulgent without becoming too heavy?  
3\. Does it feel distinctly Mediterranean rather than generic comfort food?  
4\. Is the nduja flavor memorable enough to become a signature element?

\---

\# Thinking Hierarchy

When generating concepts, prioritize in this order:

1\. Dish Principles  
2\. Menu Principles  
3\. Pantry Strategy  
4\. User Brief  
5\. Existing Menu Knowledge  
6\. Pantry Knowledge

The AI should never violate higher-level principles in order to satisfy lower-level inputs.

\---

\# Concept Evaluation Rule

Every concept should satisfy three questions:

1\. Why does this belong at Salted Olive?  
2\. Why would guests order it?  
3\. Why is it worth prototyping?

If any of these questions cannot be answered convincingly, the concept should be revised.

\---

\# Anti-Patterns

Avoid:

\- Generic ChatGPT recipe ideas  
\- Overly complicated concepts  
\- Fine-dining experimentation for its own sake  
\- Concepts that ignore Mediterranean identity  
\- Concepts that merely copy references  
\- Concepts that cannot explain why they matter

The objective is not novelty.

The objective is meaningful, testable concepts that strengthen the menu.
