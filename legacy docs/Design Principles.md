# **SALTED OLIVE PRODUCT DEVELOPMENT PRINCIPLES**

## **Purpose**

Salted Olive is a Mediterranean-style bistro built around a proprietary pantry of sauces, spice blends, dressings, oils, condiments, and future retail products.

These principles exist to guide product development decisions, ensuring that new dishes strengthen the brand, support commercial objectives, and contribute to a cohesive menu.

The principles are organised into three levels:

1. Dish Principles  
2. Menu Principles  
3. Pantry Strategy

Each level serves a different purpose and should be applied accordingly.

---

# **LEVEL 1: DISH PRINCIPLES**

## **Every Dish Must Fulfil These Principles**

These are the non-negotiables.

If a dish fails any of these principles, it should not proceed to customer testing or menu launch.

---

## **1\. Mediterranean First**

Every dish must have a recognisable Mediterranean foundation.

Contemporary twists are encouraged, but the underlying identity should remain Mediterranean.

Question:

If the contemporary twist was removed, would this still feel Mediterranean?

Examples:

* Crispy chicken with mint honey  
* Spanakopita-inspired pot pie  
* Harissa steak frites

---

## **2\. Bistro Format**

Every dish should be rooted in a familiar and approachable bistro format.

Examples:

* Roast chicken  
* Steak frites  
* Croquettes  
* Gratin  
* Salad  
* Soup  
* Sandwich

Guests should immediately understand what they are ordering.

The innovation should come from flavour and execution, not confusion.

---

## **3\. Pantry-Led**

Every dish must prominently feature at least one Salted Olive pantry product.

Examples:

* Hot Honey  
* Mint Honey  
* Salted Olive Dip  
* Harissa  
* Chicken Spice Rub  
* Eggplant Rub  
* Future sauces, dressings, pickles, and spice blends

The pantry product should contribute meaningfully to the dish, not simply appear as a garnish.

---

## **4\. Comfort Over Cleverness**

Salted Olive serves food that is warm, generous, and craveable.

The goal is repeat orders, not novelty for novelty's sake.

Question:

Would someone order this again next month?

If not, the concept should be reconsidered.

---

## **5\. Operationally Realistic**

Every dish must be executable using:

* Available ingredients  
* Reliable suppliers  
* Existing equipment  
* Existing team capabilities

No dish should depend on fragile sourcing, highly specialised ingredients, or excessive operational complexity.

---

# **LEVEL 2: MENU PRINCIPLES**

## **These Principles Apply To The Menu As A Whole**

Not every dish needs to fulfil these principles.

The menu collectively should.

---

## **1\. The Menu Must Showcase The Pantry**

Guests should encounter Salted Olive pantry products throughout their experience.

The menu should create repeated exposure to proprietary flavours across:

* Starters  
* Mains  
* Desserts  
* Beverages

The objective is to build familiarity with the Salted Olive flavour ecosystem.

---

## **2\. The Menu Must Balance Business Objectives**

The menu should contain a mix of:

### **Revenue Drivers**

Broad appeal items designed to sell consistently.

### **Margin Drivers**

Items designed to generate strong profitability.

### **Brand Drivers**

Items that strengthen identity and create conversation.

### **VIP Drivers**

Higher-ticket items designed to attract and delight premium guests.

Not every dish requires a business objective.

The menu does.

---

## **3\. The Menu Must Encourage Sharing**

Salted Olive is a social dining restaurant.

The menu should contain enough:

* Shared starters  
* Shared mains  
* Centrepiece dishes  
* Group-friendly items

to encourage communal dining experiences.

---

## **4\. The Menu Must Balance Familiarity And Discovery**

The menu should provide both comfort and curiosity.

### **Familiar Anchors**

Examples:

* Roast chicken  
* Steak  
* Comfort dishes  
* Classic bistro formats

### **Discovery Items**

Examples:

* Seasonal specials  
* New pantry products  
* Test Kitchen creations

Guests should feel comfortable ordering while still having reasons to return.

---

## **5\. The Menu Must Build Pantry Equity**

Over time, guests should begin recognising Salted Olive pantry products as signature flavour systems.

The goal is for guests to become familiar with flavours such as:

* Mint Honey  
* Hot Honey  
* Harissa  
* Salted Olive Dip  
* Future pantry products

The long-term objective is for guests to seek out Salted Olive flavours, not just individual dishes.

---

# **LEVEL 3: PANTRY STRATEGY**

## **Long-Term Innovation Direction**

These principles guide future product development priorities.

---

## **1\. Build Proprietary Flavour Systems**

Salted Olive develops:

* Sauces  
* Spice blends  
* Dressings  
* Pickles  
* Condiments  
* Oils

before developing dishes.

The flavour system is the intellectual property.

The dish is the delivery vehicle.

---

## **2\. One Product, Multiple Uses**

Every pantry product should ideally appear across multiple touchpoints.

Examples:

### **Mint Honey**

* Main dishes  
* Side dishes  
* Beverages  
* Retail jars  
* Hampers

The objective is to maximise usefulness and strengthen brand recognition.

---

## **3\. Build From Core Ingredients**

Product development should prioritise ingredients that support multiple menu applications and future retail opportunities.

Core ingredients include:

* Olive oil  
* Honey  
* Lemon  
* Garlic  
* Chilli  
* Yogurt  
* Herbs  
* Sesame  
* Eggplant  
* Olives

Preference should be given to ingredients that are:

* Accessible  
* Consistently available  
* Flexible  
* Scalable

Avoid building the pantry around ingredients that are difficult to source or difficult to replicate.

---

# **Product Development Filter**

Before entering R\&D, every idea should answer the following questions:

1. What is the bistro format?  
2. What is the Mediterranean foundation?  
3. Which pantry product is the hero?  
4. Why would a guest order this?  
5. Is it operationally realistic?  
6. How does it contribute to the menu?  
7. How does it strengthen the Salted Olive pantry?

If these questions cannot be answered clearly, the idea should remain in the Idea Bank until further development.
